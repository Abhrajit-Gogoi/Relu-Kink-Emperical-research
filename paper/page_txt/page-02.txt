Related Work

Nonsmooth automatic differentiation. Bolte and Pauwels [2021] introduce conservative setvalued fields and path-differentiable functions as the mathematical framework for AD across nondifferentiable compositions. Lee et al. [2020] formalize piecewise-analytic functions and distinguish
intensional from extensional derivatives. Lee et al. [2023] analyze AD correctness over discrete floatingpoint grids, showing that bias-free normalization and attention layers can fail well-structuredness
conditions. Park et al. [2024] prove that AD returns a valid Clarke subdifferential when minibatch
size is one with distinct biases (Theorem 1), and extend this to arbitrary minibatch sizes with
consistent convex-combination weights (Theorem 4).
Finite-precision AD pitfalls. Bertoin et al. [2021] first demonstrated numerically that the
proxy slope ReLU′ (0) influences MNIST backpropagation, reporting a ∼40% FP32 bifurcation weight
proportion; their erratum corrects a chain-rule implementation bug while stating the proportions are
unchanged. Hückelheim et al. [2023] survey AD pitfalls, framing low-precision ReLU differentiation
as the intersection of control-flow branch, chaotic dynamics, and discrete-representation pitfalls.
Yang et al. [2023] present ∇Fuzz, a differential tester that exposes AD discrepancies triggered by
non-differentiable kinks and finite-precision rounding. Martorella et al. [2023] independently confirm
the MNIST MLP trajectory bifurcation and Monte Carlo volume benchmarks.
Normalization and activations in low precision. Ba et al. [2016] introduce LayerNorm;
Zhang and Sennrich [2019] introduce RMSNorm, which performs strictly positive scaling without
mean subtraction. Hendrycks and Gimpel [2016] introduce GELU, whose derivative at zero is 0.5
continuously. Salmani et al. [2024] show that explicit control of normalization reductions matters in
low-precision Transformers, motivating our explicit norm modules.
