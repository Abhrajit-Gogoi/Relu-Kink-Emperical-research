Baseline Reproduction Discrepancy Analysis

The Stage-2 baseline reproduction check (NeurIPS 2021 MLP protocol, CPU, PyTorch 2.14.1+cu130)
measures an FP32 bifurcation weight proportion of 0.06 (12/200 initializations; 12 of 20,000 impacted
pairs) in the Monte Carlo arm with M = 200 and Q = 100, versus 0.025 (5/200) for the same FP32
NoNorm bias-on cell in Experiment 1. The 0.06 figure comes from the Stage-2 Monte Carlo cell,
not from the 10 trajectory seeds: the trajectory seeds themselves stay at L1 distance 0 for all 200
steps except where an exact zero occurs, and the median k ⋆ remains 201. The discrepancy arises
because the Stage-2 script scores 12,800 images on CPU with one thread, whereas Bertoin et al.
[2021] score the full training set on GPU; the Stage-2 bias draw uses the nn.Linear default while
Bertoin et al. [2021] do not state a bias law; and the Stage-2 chain rule multiplies the incoming
gradient by the proxy slope, whereas the original (pre-erratum) implementation assigned the slope
directly. None of these procedural differences alone explains the gap from Bertoin et al. [2021]’s
published ∼35–45% FP32 bifurcation proportion. A full-epoch extrapolation of the measured CPU
pair rate (12/20,000 = 0.0006) over Q = 469 batches of 128 gives 1 − (1 − 0.0006)469 ≈ 0.245, still
below the 35–45% band. The discrepancy is documented rather than tuned away.

Discussion

The four hypotheses are supported as follows. H1: Exact-zero collisions rise as the mantissa
narrows—BF16 (p̂0 = 2.99 × 10−5 ) exceeds FP16 (3.80 × 10−6 ), which exceeds FP32 (2.54 × 10−9 )
and FP64 (0)—because BF16 pairs FP32’s 8-bit exponent range with only a 7-bit mantissa, making
subtractive cancellation w⊤ x + b = 0.0 hit exact zero 23 = 8× more coarsely than FP16 despite
FP16’s wider mantissa. H2: LayerNorm’s mean subtraction (xi − µ) centers pre-activations at zero
and concentrates mass at the kink, raising p̂0 relative to RMSNorm’s strictly positive scaling; this
persists even with bias off (1.12 × 10−4 vs 1.13 × 10−4 for LayerNorm), confirming that centering,
not additive bias, drives exact-zero collisions. Both norms buffer test-accuracy loss relative to
unnormalized networks. H3: Twin SGD runs stay bitwise-equal until the first exact zero; Topology
C’s identity skip path Id bypasses the kink entirely (median k ⋆ = 201 in BF16), preserving gradient
flow, whereas Topology B’s on-highway residual ReLU places the second ReLU directly on the residual
sum (median k ⋆ = 1), allowing the Jacobian product to vanish. H4: GELU (GELU′ (0) = 0.5)
